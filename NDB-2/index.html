On this very day two years ago, the New Development Bank's (NDB) Independent Evaluation Office (IEO) came into existence and started its activities under the leadership of its first Director General (DG), Mr. Ashwani K. Muthoo. The vision of the Board of Directors and the NDB Management was to have an independent evaluation function responsible for promoting accountability, learning, and strengthening transparency and governance, with the broader aim of enhancing the Bank's results and impact. The establishment of IEO sent a strong signal to the international development community of the organization's seriousness in pursuing high standards and excellence in its operations and activities.
It takes time to consolidate any new function in an organization, and still more needs to be done; but in these two years, a lot of progress has been made. Arguably, the most important achievements are the advances that have been made in promoting an evaluation, results and learning culture across the Bank and its work in its member countries. This is particularly important given that culture change in any field or organization is a continuous process that requires thorough commitment and efforts transcending horizontal and vertical boundaries.
This has been possible thanks to the unwavering support of the Board, the President and the Bank’s Management and staff, who have recognized the value that IEO can generate for the organization and shown keen interest in its work.
IEO has also put in place key evaluation policies and strategies, following internationally recognized methodologies - though carefully customized to the NDB context - that have enabled it to conduct high quality and timely evaluations. In two years, IEO has completed eight evaluation reports, devoted significant attention to knowledge-sharing and outreach including by establishing a dedicated space on the NDB website (see here), promoted partnerships with a range of institutions in member countries and beyond, and supported countries in evaluation capacity development. The establishment of the High-Level Evaluation Advisory Committee and the Inter-Departmental Working Group on Evaluation have been instrumental in sharing knowledge, reviewing core documents and providing guidance that have been invaluable in the journey thus far. They are expected to increasingly play a prominent role in the further evolution of IEO moving forward.
In the near future, IEO will complete its first Evaluation Manual, detailing how evaluations are performed, roles, responsibilities and much more; and finalize several key evaluation reports such as those on the Bank's policy framework and project life cycle. At the end of 2024, it will issue the first ever Report on NDB's Development Effectiveness and initiate a comprehensive evaluation of the Bank's Ten Years of Activities, to be finalized in mid-2025. The work agenda is indeed huge and exciting, and we look forward to strengthened engagement and partnerships to take it forward. But for now, as IEO moves into its third year, let us celebrate this moment of pride and joy for the entire Bank.
